About Judy

Judy Wright

Judy Wright is a foster mother, caregiver, and advocate whose life has been shaped by the demanding, ordinary work of staying.

After raising four children, Judy and her now-late husband John entered Michigan’s foster care system. Over twelve years, their home became a place of readiness for fifty-five children, many of them medically fragile. Judy learned to advocate inside systems that often failed to see children clearly, and to love without guarantees.

In 2012, they were named Michigan Foster Parents of the Year by the Michigan Supreme Court. Their work continues through the John and Judy Wright Scholarship for Foster Children, established with the Ennis Center to support vulnerable infants in need of specialized care.

Just Hold Her is her debut memoir, drawn directly from the spiral notebooks kept during a two-year medical vigil. A portion of the book’s proceeds supports their legacy scholarship. She writes regularly at Judy’s Notebook on Substack (opens in new tab).

The Book

Just Hold Her

A Foster Mother’s Memoir of Medicine, Caregiving, and the Long Nights Together

When Judy Wright was asked to take in a medically fragile infant not expected to survive, she began a vigil she documented in spiral notebooks over 674 days. Just Hold Her reconstructs that experience from the record she kept in real time, tracing the collision between daily caregiving, medical authority, foster care, and the ordinary work of remaining present.

For interviews and features

Judy can speak about foster care, medically complex caregiving, attachment, advocacy inside institutions, the documentary role of personal notebooks, and what happens when a caregiver stays after everyone else has stopped expecting a child to live.

250 words — feature articles, advance review coverage, extended editorial listings

“Would you, please… just hold her until she dies?” That was the call Judy Wright received when she was asked to take in a medically fragile infant the system did not expect to survive. The baby was two months old, four pounds, blind, deaf, insulin-dependent, and tethered to monitors. She arrived with twenty-seven diagnoses and no plan beyond placement. They did not ask Wright to save her. They asked her to stay. Just Hold Her is the true story of what followed: a long medical vigil inside Michigan’s foster care system, reconstructed from the spiral notebooks Judy kept in real time as she tracked feedings, glucose readings, injections, alarms, and the changing patterns of a fragile child’s body. At the center of the memoir is one persistent question Judy refused to stop asking: What about breast milk? This is not a rescue story. It is a witness story — a literary memoir about foster care, medical bureaucracy, maternal presence, and the cost of remaining available when no one can promise a survivable ending. Judy and her late husband, John, fostered fifty-five children over twelve years in Michigan, many of them medically fragile. In 2012, they were recognized as Michigan Foster Parents of the Year by the Michigan Supreme Court. Their legacy also includes the John & Judy Wright Scholarship for Foster Children. Just Hold Her is Judy Wright’s debut memoir. Published by Held Close Books. Ebook release April 15, 2026. Print release June 1, 2026.
